# App Store creative assets

Bottom line: the product page **Header** and **Search Results** images are
uploaded and placed. They are generated by
`scripts/make_creative_assets.py` and pushed by
`scripts/appstore_creative_assets.py`. Neither is a screenshot, and neither
lives on the screenshot gallery.

## What these are

Apple's creative assets sit above the screenshots on the product page, and
behind the app's row in search results. Unlike previews and screenshots they
do **not** have to show the app in use — Apple's guidance is that a header
carries "a single, clear idea" for a first-time visitor, while a search
results image states the app's purpose at a glance.

So the two are deliberately different:

| Asset | Size | Ratio | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Header | 3840 × 1646 | 21:9 | A fan of five covers on the ember background. Brand, not UI. |
| Search Results | 3840 × 2560 | 3:2 | Three real screens — library, player, home. |

Both are PNG with **no alpha channel**, which Apple rejects outright. Both
use the dedicated size for their placement rather than the 5244 × 2950
universal size, so neither is centre-cropped on the way in.

## Rules that constrain the artwork

Taken from Apple's asset best practices, not guessed:

- Text is allowed, but it should be "a short phrase that enhances your visual
  rather than describes it". Both assets carry one line and a subhead.
- No pricing, no discounts, no URLs, no copyright symbols.
- No logos or references to other platforms or marketplaces.
- No Apple-designated recognitions (Editor's Choice and the like) — we have
  none, and claiming one is a rejection.
- Everything must be 4+ appropriate even though the app is rated higher.

## Regenerating

```bash
python3 scripts/make_creative_assets.py
python3 scripts/appstore_creative_assets.py            # dry run
python3 scripts/appstore_creative_assets.py --apply --place
```

The upload script reads specs from `/v1/appAssetLibraryRefData` and matches
them on the file's own pixel dimensions, so a re-render at any other
supported size still finds its spec without editing the script. It is
idempotent: an asset already in the library by file name is reused rather
than uploaded twice.

Credentials come from `ASC_ISSUER_ID`, `ASC_KEY_ID` and `ASC_KEY_PATH`. The
`.p8` is never copied into the repo and `*.p8` is gitignored.

## How the API fits together

Three resources, and the distinction matters:

1. `appAssetLibraries` — one per app, and its id **is** the app id.
2. `appAssetLibraryImages` — the uploaded file. Reserve with `category:
   CREATIVE_ASSETS` and an `assetLibrary` relationship, PUT the chunks from
   `uploadOperations`, then `PATCH … uploaded: true`.
3. `appAssetLibraryPlacements` — what actually puts an asset on a surface.
   A placement is scoped to an **`appStoreVersionLocalization`**, not to the
   app, and Apple allows exactly one image per creative placement.

An asset can sit in the library with no placement, which is what the web UI
leaves behind if you upload without saving. Reading `state` on the image is
therefore not enough — check the placement is `ACTIVE`.

## Not checked

- Apple draws the app icon, name and buttons over the product page header.
  The exact safe area lives in Apple's Figma/Sketch templates, which were
  not downloaded, so the composition keeps its focal point centred and its
  margins generous rather than matching a template exactly. Use the
  **Preview** button in App Store Connect before submitting.
- Creative assets are only displayed to users on iOS 27 / iPadOS 27 and
  later. They are harmless on earlier versions, but they will not be seen.
